Equalising the receive and transmit picture areas

doc-id sstvae-layout-2026-08-01 · branch feat/ui-polish at 6754b5d · measured from your 2026-08-01 screenshot (1360 px window, pane titles and both 4:3 boxes already landed); projections computed for the same width

Where it stands, measured

Pinning both boxes to 4:3 already pulled transmit down from ~880 px to 641. What remains is a 1.45× gap, and it is the product of two independent decisions that compound:

Pane widthPicture widthWhy
Receive~470 px443 pxSplitter stretch 2. The preview is nearly the whole pane, so the pane width is the picture width.
Transmit~875 px641 pxSplitter stretch 3, less the ~195 px side panel (Picture / Overlay / Selected item).

So the fix is not simply "swap the stretch factors". Even at 1:1 the transmit canvas keeps a ~195 px tool column that receive does not have, so an even split still leaves receive ahead by exactly that column — which is fine by your priority, but it means what each option really decides is what happens to that column. That is what separates them.

Your point about priority is the design premise here: on the air the received picture is the thing you cannot get back, and the transmit composition is something you are constructing and already know. All three options below give receive at least parity.

Option A — Symmetric columns, waterfall across the top

The waterfall leaves the receive pane and becomes a full-width strip above both. Below it the window is two identical columns: picture, status line, controls. The transmit tool column moves under its canvas as a compact row.

For: perfectly symmetric, so "which is which" is answered by position as well as by title. Both pictures beat today's transmit canvas. The waterfall goes from 443 px to ~1340, so the carriers are properly resolved instead of crowded into a third of the window.

Against: the biggest change — the waterfall moves out of ReceivePanel into MainWindow, and the transmit tools have to be redesigned from a vertical column into a horizontal row. The "Selected item" property form (Text / Align / Size / Rotation / Colour) does not fit a single row, so it becomes a popover, a collapsible strip, or a dialog.

Cost: largest. Touches the window, both panels, and needs a new home for the item properties.

Option B — Equal split, transmit tools collapse

The window stays as it is structurally. The splitter goes to 1:1, and the transmit side panel becomes a collapsible drawer — a narrow strip of icon buttons by default, expanding to today's full column when you are actually composing.

For: far smaller change than A — the waterfall stays put, no panel is restructured, and nothing is redesigned. The asymmetry becomes a thing you opt into for as long as you need it, rather than a permanent tax on the receive side. Equal by default is exactly what you asked for.

Against: icon-only buttons need tooltips and are less discoverable than the current labelled column — and this project's own rules are wary of icon-only controls. The canvas also changes width when the drawer opens, so a composition you were eyeing reflows underneath you.

Cost: moderate. One collapsible container in TransmitPanel, plus icons. No change to MainWindow beyond the stretch factors.

Option C — Receive-favoured split, tools stay put

The smallest possible change that fixes the complaint: reverse and widen the stretch to 3:2 in receive's favour, and cap the transmit side panel's width so it stops growing with the pane. Nothing moves; nothing is redesigned.

For: an afternoon's work, no redesign, no new interaction to learn, nothing to get wrong. The received picture becomes the largest thing in the window, which matches what it is for. Capping the tool column is a one-line setMaximumWidth and stops the transmit pane wasting width on stretched buttons.

Against: does not achieve equal — it overshoots hard. At 330 px the transmit canvas is smaller than today's receive preview, so placing a callsign accurately gets harder than it currently is. A gentler 5:4 would give ~700 / ~460, which is closer to your "same side by side" than 3:2 is. It also leaves the waterfall at ~800 px, which A would have fixed.

Cost: smallest. Two stretch factors and a maximum width.

My recommendation is A, and the waterfall is the reason rather than the pictures. All three fix what you raised; only A also fixes something you have not complained about yet but will — the spectrum display is the one widget in this app whose whole job is resolving detail across a frequency axis, and it is currently doing it in a quarter of the window. Symmetry also makes the two halves self-evident in a way titles only approximate.

If A's cost is not worth it right now, C is the honest interim — but at 5:4 rather than 3:2. 5:4 gives ~700 / ~460, which is genuinely close to "the same side by side" and still puts receive ahead; 3:2 overshoots to 800 / 330 and makes the composer worse than the preview is today. Either way it is a two-line change that does not close the door on A.

I would skip B: the icon drawer trades a discoverability problem for a width problem, and this project's own rules are already sceptical of icon-only controls.
